Reference 02 / trip card

Where to see them

The permanent things, the lucky things, and the “check before you go” things.

Museums rotate their collections and close between shows. Use this as a route, not a guarantee. The one-line “see” note tells you what to look for when you get there.

Snapshot: 9 November 2026 Dates below are the ones advertised on 9 November 2026. Always re-check the venue’s own site before booking trains or timed tickets — especially for Kusama, whose memorial exhibitions are still being announced.
Stop 1Tokyo — Kusama Museum, 21_21 DESIGN SIGHT, Dover Street Market Ginza, the Aoyama fashion district.
Stop 2Matsumoto — Kusama’s hometown museum, a day trip from Tokyo.
Stop 3Naoshima — the pumpkin, Benesse House, Chichu; a ferry and a full day.
Stop 4Aomori / Hirosaki — the Aomori Dog and Nara’s northern landscape; a flight or shinkansen from Tokyo.
Stop 5Kyoto / Osaka — Murakami’s Kyoto exhibition history, the Kyoto Costume Institute, and the Kansai museums.

Tokyo

Shinjuku · Tokyo

Yayoi Kusama Museum

A five-floor museum devoted to Kusama, with one thematic exhibition at a time and a rooftop view. Timed entry; closed Monday–Wednesday.

See: The Light Spreads Out, and Glittering Things Start Appearing All Around Me, 8 October 2026 – 31 January 2027. Look for: the late paintings’ eyes and profiles, and the way the building compresses you before a room opens out.

Tokyo Midtown · Roppongi

21_21 DESIGN SIGHT

The design museum Miyake co-founded in 2007, designed by Tadao Ando. It is not a fashion museum; it is the best place to see the design culture Miyake came from.

See: whatever is on — the building and the exhibition design are the point. Look for: the “piece of cloth” logic in the way exhibitions are built from simple materials.

Ginza · Tokyo

Dover Street Market Ginza

Kawakubo’s retail experiment: a department store as a changing installation, with Comme des Garçons and a rotating cast of guest designers. The Ginza store opened in 2008.

See: the current CDG lines and the store’s “beautiful chaos” layout. Look for: how the clothes are displayed as objects, not outfits.

Aoyama / Marunouchi · Tokyo

Issey Miyake, Comme des Garçons, Y’s, Y-3

The Aoyama district has the Issey Miyake flagship and the CDG Aoyama store; Marunouchi and Ginza have the department-store concessions. Y’s and Y-3 have their own stores.

See: Pleats Please, A-POC-derived pieces, and the current Yohji silhouette. Look for: the cut, the weight, and how the garment moves when you pick it up.

Roppongi · Tokyo

Mori Art Museum / Kaikai Kiki Gallery

Mori Art Museum hosted Murakami’s The 500 Arhats in 2015–16 and remains the best large-scale contemporary art stop in Tokyo. Kaikai Kiki Gallery, in Motoazabu, shows Murakami’s artists and editions.

See: whatever is current; check both sites. Look for: the studio-system scale — many hands, one signature.

Matsumoto

Matsumoto · Nagano

Matsumoto City Museum of Art

Kusama’s hometown museum, with a permanent collection that runs from the early drawings to the late My Eternal Soul paintings. The building is a short bus ride from Matsumoto Station; the castle is nearby.

See: Kusama: The place for my soul, 19 September – 6 December 2026, plus the year-round collection. Look for: the early work before the dots became a brand — the watercolours, the plant forms, the family nursery.

Naoshima

Naoshima · Kagawa

Benesse Art Site Naoshima

The island where Kusama’s yellow Pumpkin (1994) sits on a pier, with the Seto Inland Sea behind it. Benesse House, Chichu, and the Lee Ufan Museum are the other anchors. Ferries from Uno or Takamatsu.

See: Pumpkin at the pier; the pumpkin is outdoors and free. Look for: the scale shift — the dots are the same motif as the paintings, but your body is now inside the composition.

Aomori and Hirosaki

Aomori · Aomori

Aomori Museum of Art

The museum holds more than 170 Nara works and the 8.5 m Aomori-ken (Aomori Dog) (2005), a concrete dog that appears to be emerging from the ground. The collection also includes Munakata Shiko and Narita Tohl.

See: the Aomori Dog outdoors; the collection exhibition. Nara may not be on display during a given project, so check the museum’s calendar. Look for: the dog’s eyes — closed, content, faintly melancholy.

Hirosaki · Aomori

Hirosaki Museum of Contemporary Art / city sites

Nara’s AtoZ Memorial Dog stands in Hirosaki, the city where he was born. The Hirosaki Museum of Contemporary Art (HIROSAKI MOCA) is the other contemporary stop; check its Nara programme and dates.

See: AtoZ Memorial Dog; the Hirosaki show (2026 dates advertised 5 June – 15 November). Look for: the difference between the white Aomori Dog and the slightly earlier Hirosaki dog — one is a monument, the other a companion.

Kyoto and Osaka

Kyoto · Kyoto

Kyoto City Kyocera Museum of Art

The venue for Takashi Murakami: Mononoke Kyoto (2024), a large survey that put Superflat back in the city of traditional painting. Check the current programme; the museum’s architecture alone is worth the stop.

See: whatever is current. Look for: the argument between Edo painting and anime — the same flat picture plane, different economies.

Kyoto · Kyoto

Kyoto Costume Institute

A research collection rather than a permanent public museum. It is the deep archive for Japanese and Western fashion, and the source of many of the garments you see in touring shows.

See: check the KCI site for current exhibitions and access. Look for: construction from the inside — the cook’s tour of a garment’s seams and pleats.

If you are not going to Japan

The deepest holdings are not all in Japan. These are the rooms worth a detour.

Hong Kong

M+

A major museum of visual culture in Asia, with strong Kusama, Nara, and Murakami holdings and a habit of showing them together.

See: the M+ collections; the Kusama Infinity Mirror Room when installed. Look for: the regional argument — “Japan” looks different from Hong Kong.

London

Tate Modern / V&A

Tate Modern for Kusama’s Infinity Mirror Rooms and the international canon; the V&A for the fashion collections and the best object-level labels in the business.

See: Kusama at Tate; Miyake, Kawakubo, and Yamamoto in the V&A fashion galleries. Look for: the difference between a museum’s “art” frame and a design museum’s “object” frame.

Paris

Palais Galliera / Musée des Arts Décoratifs

The fashion museums. Miyake’s 1988 A-ŪN exhibition at the Musée des Arts Décoratifs was a turning point in how fashion was shown.

See: current fashion exhibitions; check the Galliera’s schedule. Look for: how the French state frames Japanese fashion — as art, as industry, or both.

New York

The Met / MoMA / Brooklyn Museum

The Met’s Costume Institute for Kawakubo, Miyake, and Yamamoto; MoMA for Kusama and the design collection; the Brooklyn Museum for Murakami’s ©Murakami (2008).

See: the permanent collections and whatever is on. Look for: the wall text — the Met and MoMA disagree about whether these objects are art or design.

Amsterdam

Stedelijk Museum

A major Kusama retrospective, 11 September 2026 – 17 January 2027, organised with Fondation Beyeler and Museum Ludwig.

See: the retrospective. Look for: the early work in depth — the 1950s and 1960s are the revelation.

Melbourne

NGV International

The NGV’s Collecting Comme holdings are a major Kawakubo collection, with more than sixty-five examples.

See: the fashion galleries. Look for: the 1980s pieces next to the 1997 “lumps” — the through-line is the body, not the season.

Before you go — the five-minute check

  • Is the museum open? Many close Mondays; Kusama Museum closes Monday–Wednesday.
  • Is the work on display, or is it in storage? Permanent collections rotate.
  • Do you need a timed ticket? Kusama, Naoshima, and the Aomori Dog corridor can be timed or closed.
  • Is photography allowed? Assume no in Kusama rooms; check the sign before you raise the phone.
  • Is there a catalogue? Buy it at the show; the best essays are often not online.

Sources: venue websites linked above; Tokyo Art Beat for current listings.
