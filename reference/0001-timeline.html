Reference 01 / print me

One line, six lives

The war, the boom, the bubble, and the moment Japanese fashion took Paris.

Use this to place a work before you read its label. The dates are the spine; the notes are the argument.

  1. 1945
    The war ends. Hiroshima and Nagasaki are bombed in August.
    Context · Japan
    Issey Miyake, aged seven, survives Hiroshima; his mother dies. The occupation begins. Every artist in the first half of this course grows up inside this aftermath.
  2. 1957–58
    Kusama flies to Seattle, then New York; the Infinity Nets begin.
    Yayoi Kusama
    She had written to Georgia O’Keeffe for advice. In New York she paints thousands of small loops on dark grounds — the first fully mature statement of self-obliteration. Lesson 01 →
  3. 1959
    Kusama’s first New York solo at Brata Gallery. Yoshitomo Nara is born in Hirosaki.
    Kusama · Nara
    The two ends of the course overlap: Kusama is already in New York when Nara is born in Aomori, 300 miles north of Tokyo.
  4. 1962
    Takashi Murakami is born in Tokyo.
    Takashi Murakami
    He grows up in the high-growth years, with anime on television and the Tokyo Olympics on the horizon. Lesson 06 →
  5. 1964
    Tokyo Olympics; Miyake graduates from Tama Art University.
    Context · Miyake
    The Olympics announce Japan’s post-war reconstruction. Miyake leaves for Paris the following year.
  6. 1965–66
    Kusama makes Infinity Mirror Room—Phalli’s Field and stages Narcissus Garden at Venice without an invitation.
    Yayoi Kusama
    The room and the mirror balls are the two templates for everything she does later: infinity as an environment, and the artwork as a situation you enter. Lesson 01 →
  7. 1969
    Rei Kawakubo founds Comme des Garçons.
    Rei Kawakubo
    The label begins in Tokyo; the Paris debut comes twelve years later. Lesson 03 →
  8. 1970–73
    Miyake Design Studio opens; Miyake shows in Paris from 1973.
    Issey Miyake
    Miyake is the first of the three fashion designers to be seen in Paris. His subject is not “Japan” but cloth, movement, and daily life. Lesson 02 →
  9. 1972
    Yohji Yamamoto founds Y’s in Tokyo.
    Yohji Yamamoto
    The label starts as a women’s business. The oversized black silhouette that becomes famous is still a decade away. Lesson 04 →
  10. 1973–77
    Kusama returns to Japan; within four years she enters a Tokyo psychiatric hospital.
    Yayoi Kusama
    She lives there for the rest of her life, working in a studio across the street. The “comeback” is not a return to health; it is a working arrangement.
  11. 1981
    Kawakubo and Yamamoto debut in Paris in the same season.
    Rei Kawakubo · Yohji Yamamoto
    Oversized, black, asymmetric, apparently unfinished. The French press is scandalised; the “Japanese fashion” category is born. Miyake is already showing in Paris. Lesson 03 → Lesson 04 →
  12. 1988
    Nara moves to Düsseldorf; Miyake begins his pleating experiments.
    Yoshitomo Nara · Issey Miyake
    Nara spends twelve years in Germany, most of it alone; the solitary child becomes his subject. Miyake works out how to pleat a finished garment.
  13. 1993
    Pleats Please launches. Kusama represents Japan at the Venice Biennale.
    Issey Miyake · Yayoi Kusama
    Miyake’s pleats become a mass line; Kusama, who staged a guerrilla action at Venice in 1966, is finally inside the official pavilion.
  14. 1994
    Kusama’s Pumpkin is installed on Naoshima. Nara moves to Cologne.
    Yayoi Kusama · Yoshitomo Nara
    The pumpkin becomes the most photographed public sculpture in Japan. Nara spends six more years in Germany before returning to Japan in 2000. Lesson 05 →
  15. 1997
    Kawakubo shows Body Meets Dress, Dress Meets Body.
    Rei Kawakubo
    Padded lumps distort the model’s body; gingham makes the whole thing look domestic. The collection is still called “Lumps and Bumps” in the press. Lesson 03 →
  16. 1998–99
    Miyake and Dai Fujiwara develop A-POC; the line launches in 1999.
    Issey Miyake
    A tube of cloth knitted from a single thread, cut by the wearer. The garment becomes a system rather than a shape. Lesson 02 →
  17. 2000
    Murakami publishes the Superflat catalogue; Nara returns to Japan for Don’t Mind, If You Forget Me.
    Takashi Murakami · Yoshitomo Nara
    Murakami’s essay turns Edo painting, anime, and otaku culture into a single art-historical argument. Nara’s Yokohama show makes the big-headed children a national image. Lesson 05 → Lesson 06 →
  18. 2001
    MOCA’s Superflat exhibition; Kaikai Kiki is founded.
    Takashi Murakami
    The exhibition runs 14 January – 27 May 2001 and includes Nara, Chiho Aoshima, and others. Murakami’s studio becomes a company.
  19. 2002–03
    Kusama’s Obliteration Room; Murakami’s Louis Vuitton collaboration.
    Yayoi Kusama · Takashi Murakami
    The two poles of the course: a room completed by visitors, and a luxury collaboration that turns an artwork into a global product. Lesson 01 → Lesson 06 →
  20. 2005
    Murakami’s Little Boy at Japan Society; Nara’s Aomori-ken (Aomori Dog) is installed.
    Takashi Murakami · Yoshitomo Nara
    Murakami turns the “little boy” of the bomb and the otaku into an exhibition title. Nara’s 8.5 m concrete dog becomes a landmark in Aomori.
  21. 2007–08
    Murakami’s My Lonesome Cowboy sells for $15.2 million; ©Murakami opens at the Brooklyn Museum.
    Takashi Murakami
    The auction record makes the art-market question unavoidable. The retrospective puts the work in a museum while the merchandise is still on sale downstairs.
  22. 2010
    Murakami at Versailles; Nara’s Nobody’s Fool at Asia Society.
    Takashi Murakami · Yoshitomo Nara
    Murakami’s flowers and characters occupy the palace of the French monarchy. Nara gets his first major New York show, curated by Melissa Chiu and Miwako Tezuka.
  23. 2011
    The Tōhoku earthquake, tsunami, and Fukushima disaster.
    Context · Japan
    Nara’s work turns softer and more inward. Murakami responds with The 500 Arhats, a 100-metre painting made with a large team. The disaster is a dividing line in both careers.
  24. 2015
    The 500 Arhats opens at Mori Art Museum, Tokyo.
    Takashi Murakami
    A 3 × 100 m painting, first shown in Doha and then in Tokyo — its first showing in Japan. It is the clearest example of Murakami’s studio-as-factory model. Lesson 06 →
  25. 2017
    The Met’s Rei Kawakubo/Comme des Garçons: Art of the In-Between; the Yayoi Kusama Museum opens in Tokyo.
    Rei Kawakubo · Yayoi Kusama
    The Costume Institute devotes a full retrospective to Kawakubo. Kusama gets a five-floor museum in Shinjuku. Lesson 03 → Lesson 01 →
  26. 2019
    Nara’s Knife Behind Back sells for HK$195.7 million (about $24.9 million).
    Yoshitomo Nara
    The highest price for a Japanese artist at auction. The painting is a girl with a knife hidden behind her back — the cute/menacing formula at full scale. Lesson 05 →
  27. 2022
    Issey Miyake dies in Tokyo, aged 84.
    Issey Miyake
    The house continues under his team; the 2016 National Art Center retrospective remains the fullest account of the work. Lesson 02 →
  28. 2024
    Takashi Murakami: Mononoke Kyoto at the Kyoto City Kyocera Museum.
    Takashi Murakami
    A large survey that puts the Superflat argument back in the city of traditional painting. Around 170 works.
  29. 2026
    Kusama dies on 14 August, aged 97. Retrospectives continue in Matsumoto, Tokyo, and Amsterdam.
    Yayoi Kusama
    Matsumoto City Museum of Art: Kusama: The place for my soul, 19 September – 6 December 2026. Yayoi Kusama Museum: The Light Spreads Out…, 8 October 2026 – 31 January 2027. Lesson 01 →
